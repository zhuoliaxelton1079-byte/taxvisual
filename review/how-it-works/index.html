Return review

How the 1040 view works

A filed return is a stack of forms that each show a total and hide the path to it. The view draws the path: Form 1040 at the top, the schedules that feed each line under it, and the documents that feed the schedules at the bottom. This is how it is built, how it reads a return you give it, the checks it runs, and what it cannot tell you.

The tree

Form 1040 page 1 and page 2 are the roots. Open a line to see the schedule lines that flow into it — line 8 from Schedule 1, line 13 from Form 8995, line 19 from Schedule 8812 — and open those to reach what feeds them: Schedule C under Schedule 1, Form 4562 under Schedule C, the W-2s and the 83(b) election under wages. Select a line or a document and the page unfolds it in place, with the form or document itself, so you can read the figure where it was printed. Several can stay open at once. If you would rather have a pop-up, there is a switch for that.

The built-in sample is a fictional married-filing-jointly 2025 return, with replica source documents behind it. The board header shows its filing status and dependents.

Reading your own return

Below the sample there is a place to choose a text-based PDF of a filed return — Form 1040 and its schedules, exported from your tax software. Redact it first: names, Social Security numbers, employer numbers, addresses, bank numbers and the preparer’s details are not needed, and the page stops and asks if it still sees something that looks like an identifier.

  • Lines are recognised by their label, not their number. The form prints “This is your adjusted gross income” on line 11 in 2024 and on line 11a in 2025. The page looks for the label, finds the printed line-number box that goes with it, reads the amount beside that box, and shows you the number the form printed.
  • The tree is built from what it found. Each schedule line is placed under the Form 1040 line it feeds, following the form instructions. A blank line with nothing beneath it is left out.
  • Your pages are the documents. Each line opens the page of your own PDF it was read from, drawn in your browser. Pages the page recognises but does not read, and pages it does not recognise, are listed so you can still look at them.

The cross-checks

A line that one form carries to another must be the same number on both. With a return read, the page makes every comparison it can:

  • Carried lines — Form 1040 line 8 against Schedule 1 line 10, line 10 against Schedule 1 line 26, line 13 against Form 8995, line 19 against Schedule 8812, line 23 against Schedule 2 line 21, and the links beneath them: Schedule C into Schedule 1 and Schedule SE, Schedule SE into Schedules 1 and 2, Form 4797 into Schedule D and Schedule 1, Form 8960 into Schedule 2.
  • The arithmetic of Form 1040 — total income is the sum of the income lines, adjusted gross income is total income less adjustments, taxable income is AGI less the deductions, total tax is tax after credits plus other taxes, and the refund or the amount owed follows from payments.
  • Schedule D — the net is short-term plus long-term.

Differences within one dollar are treated as rounding. A difference is flagged on the line and listed above the tree, and it is a question, not a conclusion: it can be a rounding, a page missing from the file, two Schedule Cs where the page compares one, or a line the page has read differently from your software. When everything ties, that proves only that these lines are consistent with each other.

The forms it reads

  • Read for amounts — Form 1040 (both pages), Schedules 1, 2, A, C, D and SE, and Forms 4562, 4797, 8812, 8960 and 8995.
  • Recognised, not read — Form 8949, Schedules B, E and 3, Forms 8582 and 8959, and W-2s. They appear as pages you can open.
  • Everything else — state returns, worksheets, e-file authorisations and the like are listed as other pages.

What it cannot establish

Worth being blunt about, because these are the limits that matter:

  • It cannot tell you that anything is right. It reads what the return says and checks that it is consistent with itself. It does not recompute the tax and cannot see the documents behind a return that does not include them. A wrong figure that is wrong everywhere agrees with itself.
  • It cannot tell you that you qualify for anything. Eligibility for credits and most deductions turns on facts that never appear on the forms.
  • Reading a PDF is imperfect. It was built and tested on a 2024 return, where it read every line it covers. Other years are expected to read because lines are found by label, but they have only been tested on synthetic pages, and a PDF written by different software can split its text differently. A misread can show up as a false difference, which is why differences are worded as questions. Scanned returns have no selectable text and are refused.
  • It does not see names or addresses. Redacting them is up to you.
  • It is not tax advice, and nothing it says should drive a filing decision on its own.
