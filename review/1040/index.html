Return review · Source documents to Form 1040

From Form 1040 down to the source documents

An expandable tree, from the top down. Form 1040 page 1 and page 2 are the roots. Under each line are the schedules and lines that flow into it, and under those, the source documents that feed the schedules. Select a line or a document to unfold the documents behind it in place. The sample is a fictional married-filing-jointly 2025 return built from replica source documents. To see how the same view reads a real return, analyze a redacted PDF of your own below: it is read in your browser and never uploaded.

Analyze your own return

Choose a text-based PDF of a filed return: Form 1040 and its schedules. The page recognises each line by its printed label (so a renumbered form still reads), builds the same tree for your return, shows your own pages as the documents, and cross-checks the forms against each other. It was built and tested on a 2024 return; a layout it does not recognise is listed as an unread page rather than guessed at.

Redact it first

The page needs only the labels and amounts. Before you choose the file, remove: names, Social Security numbers and ITINs, EINs, home address, phone and email, bank routing and account numbers, and the preparer’s name, PTIN and firm. If the text still contains something that looks like one of these identifiers, the page stops and asks you to redact it.

Your file stays on this device. It is read by your browser; nothing is sent anywhere, stored, or logged. The only request the page makes for this is for the pdf.js reader from a public CDN, and it refuses to run it unless the file matches a digest pinned in the page. How to check this.

From source document to refund

Form 1040 and what feeds it

Start at the two pages of Form 1040. Open a line to see the schedules that flow into it; open those to reach the source documents. Select a line or a document to unfold it in place.

    How the amounts are built. A line that adds or subtracts other lines is computed here from those lines, so the page cannot disagree with itself. A few lines have no source document and are given: the standard deduction (filing status), the tax (a worksheet) and a credit that depends on the family (the dependent’s eligibility). They are marked “no source document”.

    Check line numbers against the forms for the year before relying on them. All names, numbers and addresses are fictional, the replicas are marked “Not an Official IRS Document”, and nothing on this page is tax advice.
